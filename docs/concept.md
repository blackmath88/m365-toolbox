# m365-toolbox — concept

## Purpose

The toolbox is not a collection of Microsoft 365 feature explainers.

It is a collection of **small interventions that reduce friction in everyday digital work**.

Each intervention should end with something changed:

- a team agreement,
- a setting,
- a cleaner structure,
- a working convention,
- a concrete next action,
- or a small experiment to try.

If a tool merely leaves somebody knowing more about Microsoft 365, it probably does not belong here.

## Core principle

> **Less friction = real solution.**

The useful question is rarely “Which Microsoft 365 feature should I use?”

The useful question is more often:

> **What is the smallest useful agreement or change we can make right now?**

That means the toolbox should prefer:

- real examples over product descriptions,
- team conventions over generic best practice,
- defaults over policy,
- small decisions over long guidance,
- actual behaviour change over training completion,
- 3–15 minute interventions over courses,
- opinionated prompts over blank-page facilitation.

## Three modes

### US

**Make working together easier.**

Team-based, usually self-facilitated. The ideal duration is around 15 minutes.

Candidate interventions:

- Where are my files? Where are our files?
- How do we communicate here?
- Our Digital House
- Paula Planner
- How do we work here?

### ME

**Make M365 work like I work.**

Short personal interventions. Usually 3–5 minutes. The person should leave with a small set of settings actually changed.

Candidate interventions:

- Notification Personality
- Family First
- Make Teams look sane
- Five defaults worth changing

### RESET

**Things accumulate. Clean them.**

Short, recurring cleanup interventions.

Candidate interventions:

- Digital Hygiene Day
- forgotten Teams / channels
- stale guests
- dead tabs and links
- abandoned Planner boards
- file graveyards

## Front door: One Friction Less

A fourth concept sits above the three modes and can eventually become the front door to the toolbox.

### One Friction Less

Start with the actual annoyance, not with Microsoft 365.

Examples:

- We could not find the latest file.
- Nobody knew whether the task was done.
- We had a meeting that should have been a message.
- Three people made their own spreadsheet.
- Nobody reads that channel.
- People get pinged outside their working hours.
- We enter the same information twice.

The intervention asks:

1. What annoyed us this week?
2. What causes the friction?
3. Is the problem clarity, ownership, too many places, missing convention, a bad default, or manual repetition?
4. What is the smallest change that would make this 20% less annoying?
5. Try it for two weeks.

This can later route people into one of the concrete toolbox interventions.

# Intervention concepts

## 1. Where are my files? Where are our files?

**Mode:** US  
**Time:** 15 minutes  
**Outcome:** 4–6 team file conventions

This is not a SharePoint-versus-OneDrive quiz.

The core discussion is **ownership**.

A useful workshop question is:

> **If you left the university tomorrow, should this thing stay?**

The team uses real examples from its own work and discusses the point where a file changes from **mine** to **ours**.

Useful product truth behind the intervention:

- files uploaded in Teams chat are stored in the sender's OneDrive;
- files uploaded to a Teams channel are stored in the Team's SharePoint-backed storage.

This gives us a real behavioural distinction instead of abstract product teaching.

### Suggested flow

**0–3 min — Show your mess**

Each person picks one real file used this week.

Ask:

- Where is it now?
- Who owns it?
- Who needs it?
- Will somebody still need it in six months?

**3–7 min — Draw the line**

Discuss:

> At what moment does MY file become OUR file?

Working heuristic:

- personal draft / scratch / temporary → mine;
- shared working material / template / minutes / team knowledge → ours.

**7–12 min — Make the rules**

Provide unfinished conventions rather than a blank page:

- Drafts live in ______.
- Once a document becomes shared team work, it moves to ______.
- Final team material lives in ______.
- We do / do not attach files to email.
- Files shared in chats are used for ______ only.
- We share links instead of copies when ______.

**12–15 min — Leave with something**

Generate a simple card that can be copied into the Team:

```text
OUR FILE RULES

01 Personal drafts start in OneDrive.
02 Shared working documents live in the Team.
03 Final templates live in [Channel / Folder].
04 Chat attachments are temporary.
05 We share links, not copies.

Agreed: [date]
Review: [date]
```

Tone idea:

> `budget_final_v3_REALLYFINAL2.xlsx`
>
> Something went wrong before we even started talking about Microsoft 365.

## 2. How do we communicate here?

**Mode:** US  
**Time:** 15 minutes  
**Outcome:** team communication conventions

Do not begin with Teams vs Outlook.

Begin with **audience, urgency and lifespan**.

### Suggested prompts

**Who needs this?**

- one person,
- a few people,
- the team,
- somebody external.

**How long should it remain useful?**

- ten minutes,
- today,
- weeks/months,
- institutional record.

**How urgent is it really?**

- whenever,
- today,
- genuinely now.

Then let the team decide what each medium means for them.

Possible default conventions:

**Chat**  
Fast and temporary. Small audience.

**Channel**  
Work that belongs to the team and should remain visible and findable.

**Email**  
External, formal or boundary-crossing asynchronous communication.

**Call**  
Actually urgent.

Useful axioms:

> **A message is not urgent because you sent it in chat.**

> **@mention means “I need your attention”, not “look, a person exists.”**

The team can override defaults. The point is to make expectations explicit.

## 3. Our Digital House

**Mode:** US  
**Time:** 15 minutes  
**Outcome:** minimal Team/channel architecture plus purpose and access rules

Use the Team as a house metaphor.

- Team = the house
- standard channel = shared room
- private channel = room with a restricted key
- shared channel = room whose membership can cut across the normal house membership

The metaphor should carry real architecture without starting from Microsoft terminology.

### Workshop questions

1. What is this house for?
2. Which rooms do we actually use?
3. Who has keys?
4. What belongs in each room?
5. Which room can disappear?

Rule:

> **Empty rooms are not organisation.**

Output:

- one-sentence Team purpose,
- minimal channel set,
- purpose per channel,
- access notes,
- one review date.

## 4. Digital Hygiene Day

**Mode:** RESET  
**Time:** 15–30 minutes  
**Outcome:** cleanup actions plus next review date

User-facing language should avoid governance jargon.

This is digital spring cleaning.

### Round 1 — PEOPLE

- Do we have at least two owners?
- Does everybody still belong here?
- Do we know who our guests are?
- Are former colleagues still members?

### Round 2 — ROOMS

For every channel:

- KEEP
- CLEAN
- ARCHIVE
- WHY DOES THIS EXIST?

The last option is deliberate.

### Round 3 — STUFF

Look for:

- duplicate folders,
- unused tabs,
- dead links,
- abandoned Planner plans,
- old project material,
- channels called `New`, `Misc`, `Old`, etc.,
- information living in the wrong place.

### Round 4 — NEXT CLEAN

Ask one final question:

> **When will we look at this Team again?**

Set a review date.

Microsoft 365 supports actual lifecycle actions such as archiving Teams/channels, so the workshop can end in concrete action rather than a list of observations.

## 5. Paula Planner

**Mode:** US / guided agent  
**Time:** 10–15 minutes  
**Outcome:** usable Planner structure plus team rules

Paula is not a Planner help bot.

She should help a team design **how it will actually use Planner**.

Planner already supplies flexible primitives: buckets, assignments, dates, labels, progress, checklists, attachments, filters and views. The value is choosing conventions.

### Paula asks

- What kind of work is this: project, recurring operations, service queue, event/campaign, mixed?
- What do you currently lose track of?
- Are buckets stages of work or workstreams?
- Who owns tasks?
- Do due dates represent real commitments?
- What regularly falls through the cracks?

A key decision:

### Workflow model

```text
INBOX → READY → DOING → WAITING → DONE
```

versus

### Workstream model

```text
COMMUNICATION
CONTENT
TECHNICAL
LOGISTICS
```

Do not mix both randomly.

### Possible generated conventions

- Task titles use **verb + outcome**.
- Every committed task has one accountable owner.
- Due dates mean commitments, not vague intentions.
- Checklists contain substeps, not hidden parallel tasks.
- Labels add a second dimension instead of duplicating buckets.
- Planner gets reviewed at a defined recurring moment.

Paula should produce a setup people can build immediately, and later potentially provision directly.

## 6. Notification Personality

**Mode:** ME  
**Time:** 3–5 minutes  
**Outcome:** a small set of Teams notification settings changed

Do not expose the settings taxonomy first.

Start with how the person wants to work.

Possible personalities:

### 24-hour full power, no shower

> I want Teams to tell me everything and I accept the consequences.

Optional feedback:

> You have chosen violence.

### Office Hours

> While I am working, be useful. Afterwards, shut up.

### Deep Worker

> People may contact me. Teams does not get to decide when I look.

### FOMO With Boundaries

> I want visibility, not interruption.

The tool translates the personality into the handful of Teams notification settings that matter: chat, channel, mentions/replies, banners/activity, followed threads and other current controls.

Principle:

> **Do not configure features. Configure how you want to work.**

`Sound Check` and `Phone Mode` are deliberately not separate tools. Their useful settings belong here or in Family First.

## 7. Family First

**Mode:** ME  
**Time:** 3 minutes  
**Outcome:** explicit work boundary in Microsoft 365

This is a tiny digital-boundary setup.

Useful Microsoft 365 capabilities include recurring working days/hours/location across Outlook/Teams, quiet time on mobile, and schedule-send behaviours.

### Suggested flow

**Step 1 — When do you actually work?**

Use the person's real work week rather than a fictional default.

**Step 2 — When should work stop arriving in your pocket?**

Set quiet time.

**Step 3 — Should colleagues be able to see your working pattern/location?**

Set the work plan appropriately.

**Step 4 — Return the favour.**

Introduce scheduled send and respectful asynchronous behaviour.

Possible closing line:

> **Work whenever suits you. Don't export your schedule to everybody else.**

## 8. Make Teams look sane

**Mode:** ME  
**Time:** 3 minutes  
**Outcome:** cleaner personal Teams navigation

Problem-led, not setting-led.

Opening question:

> **What annoys you?**

Examples:

- I can never find anything.
- Chat and channels are one giant mess.
- I hate switching between chat and channels.
- I see eighty things I do not care about.
- I only really work in six places.

Then recommend current Teams view/navigation choices such as:

- combined vs separate chat/channels,
- Favorites,
- custom sections,
- hiding inactive/noisy items,
- focusing the visible workspace.

No learning objective. Three minutes later Teams should be less irritating.

---

## 9. Five defaults worth changing

**Mode:** ME  
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Outcome:** personal baseline

Do not build a giant settings audit.

The framing is:

> Microsoft 365 has hundreds of settings. These five actually change your day.

The exact five can evolve with Teams, but candidate themes are:

1. combined vs separate chat/channels,
2. Favorites / visible workspace,
3. notification behaviour,
4. working hours,
5. quiet/focus behaviour.

This intervention should be maintained as current product behaviour changes.

## 10. How do we work here?

**Mode:** US  
**Time:** 20–25 minutes  
**Outcome:** one-page team operating card

This is the composite workshop. It should be built after the smaller interventions are proven.

It pulls together:

```text
FILES
Where are ours?

COMMUNICATION
Where do we talk?

TASKS
Where do commitments live?

AVAILABILITY
When can people reasonably expect us?

HOUSE
What spaces do we maintain?
```

Example output:

```text
HOW WE WORK

FILES
Shared work lives in the Project Team.
Personal drafts can remain in OneDrive.

COMMUNICATION
Team questions go into the relevant channel.
Chat is for short-lived coordination.
Urgent = call.

TASKS
Committed work goes into Planner.
Every task has an owner.

BOUNDARIES
Nobody is expected to respond outside their working hours.

HYGIENE
We review the Team every six months.
```

Goal: a team constitution people might actually read.

# Product rules

Every intervention should satisfy most of these:

1. **Starts from a real friction or decision.**
2. **Takes 3–15 minutes** unless deliberately a composite workshop.
3. **Uses real work examples** wherever possible.
4. **Avoids Microsoft feature taxonomy as the entry point.**
5. **Makes useful defaults visible** instead of presenting a blank page.
6. **Produces an artifact, changed setting or concrete action.**
7. **Works without login** wherever possible.
8. **Keeps tenant-specific claims clearly separated from general product behaviour.**
9. **Uses humour sparingly but memorably.**
10. **Reduces friction immediately.**

# Tone

The toolbox may be professional without sounding like Microsoft documentation.

Preferred voice:

- direct,
- compact,
- human,
- slightly dry,
- occasionally cheeky,
- never corporate transformation language for its own sake.

Good:

> Empty rooms are not organisation.

> A message is not urgent because you sent it in chat.

> What annoyed your team this week?

Avoid:

- digital maturity journeys,
- governance maturity scores,
- adoption excellence,
- feature tours,
- generic best-practice portals.

# Visual direction

Do not turn the toolbox into a generic dashboard or a Fluent UI clone.

Think more like a **deck of interventions / instruction cards / workshop recipes**.

Each intervention should foreground:

- time,
- who it is for,
- the friction,
- the question,
- what you leave with.

Examples:

```text
15 MINUTES
TO STOP LOSING FILES
```

```text
3 MINUTES
TO MAKE TEAMS BEHAVE
```

```text
TODAY
WE CLEAN UP OUR SHIT
```

```text
PAULA
WILL SORT YOUR PLANNER OUT
```

Very little decorative UI. The content and action should do the work.
